> *"A community is a loop, not a line. Students enter as learners, grow into active contributors, and return as mentors who empower the next generation."*

Every open-source community thrives on continuity. From October 2 to October 4, 2026, **[WikiClub Tech India](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/IIITH-OKI/WikiClub_Tech)**, under the umbrella of **The Open Knowledge Initiatives (OKI) at IIIT Hyderabad** and supported by the **Wikimedia Foundation**, hosted the **[Road to Wiki Graduation 2026 (The Celebration Day)](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/IIITH-OKI/Wikiclub_Tech/Road_To_Wiki/The_Celebration_Day)** at Gurugram.

The event brought together **[Road to Wiki Cohort 2026](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Road_to_Wiki/Cohort_2026)** graduates from engineering colleges across India—including students from United University—alongside dedicated mentors, Wikimedia community leaders, and industry practitioners for three days of hands-on building, knowledge sharing, and milestone celebration.

---

## The Vision: Bridging Academia and Open Source

The **[Road to Wiki](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/Road_to_Wiki/Cohort_2026)** initiative was designed with a focused mission: to bridge the gap between academic classrooms and large-scale open-source projects like **MediaWiki**. 

Through student-led initiatives like **[WikiClub Tech](https://meta.wikimedia.org/wiki/IIITH-OKI/WikiClub_Tech)**, participants gain practical experience by actively contributing to Wikimedia tools, documentation, and software libraries. 

While India holds an immense pool of technical talent, structured entry points into global open-source ecosystems are often limited for students in Tier-2 and Tier-3 institutions. Road to Wiki established transparent, guided learning tracks where participants learned:

- How the global Wikimedia technical ecosystem functions.
- The collaborative workflows behind MediaWiki, Toolhub, Phabricator, and Wikimedia REST APIs.
- Real-world engineering practices: documentation, open code reviews, test writing, and public discussion.
- How to move from consuming free software to designing, shipping, and maintaining tools for millions of global users.

## Event Overview: The Three-Day Arc

![Keynote session at Road to Wiki](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/2/2b/Keynote_speaker_from_OKI_addressing_audience_at_Road_to_Wiki_event.jpg/1280px-Keynote_speaker_from_OKI_addressing_audience_at_Road_to_Wiki_event.jpg)

The three-day celebration balanced structured engineering workshops, community discussions, and collaborative challenges.

### Day 1 · Arrival, Connections & Late-Night Builds
The opening day welcomed participants with a traditional *Swagat*, setting a collaborative tone:
- **Keynote Address ("Beyond Road to Wiki")**: Leadership from OKI and community advisers shared perspectives on building long-term open-source careers and contributing to the open-knowledge movement.
- **Networking & Icebreaker Activities**: Cohort members connected with mentors and peers from different campuses across India.
- **Robo Build Challenge**: Teams received their hardware kits, unboxed chassis components, and collaborated late into the night assembling and wiring combat robots for the upcoming competition.

---

### Day 2 · Industry Exposure, Robo War Arena & Celebration
Day 2 combined real-world software industry exposure with high-energy technical challenges:
- **Industry & Open Source at Scale**: Interactive office sessions exploring large-scale system architecture, developer operations, and the intersection of artificial intelligence with free knowledge.
- **FOSS Solution Showcase**: Highlighting tools, SDKs, and applications created by cohort participants during the program.
- **Robo War Arena Competition**: A signature event where custom-built participant robots faced off in an arena, testing mechanical design, electronics, and fast problem-solving under pressure.
- **Formal Graduation & Awards Ceremony**: Celebrating cohort milestones, project achievements, and acknowledging student leads and mentors with tokens of appreciation.

![Robo War Preparation and Assembly](https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/a/ab/Robo_War_Preparation_1.jpg/960px-Robo_War_Preparation_1.jpg)

---

### Day 3 · Checkout and Continuing the Loop
The final day focused on reflection, future roadmap planning, and sustaining community momentum:
- Discussions on post-cohort pathways: participating in Wikimedia developer grants, contributing to Phabricator workboards, and mentoring the incoming Cohort 3.
- Group photo sessions capturing cohort memories before participants headed back to their home universities.

## Core Takeaways for Aspiring Contributors

For students and developers looking to begin their journey in open source, the Road to Wiki cohort highlighted several timeless principles:

1. **Start Before You Feel Ready**: You don't need to be an expert to make your first contribution. Ask questions in public forums and learn through code reviews.
2. **Build and Ship Real Solutions**: Developing a functional tool, SDK, or bug fix teaches practical software engineering faster than passive tutorials.
3. **Open Source Is a Community Effort**: Technical infrastructure is essential, but human connection, documentation, and mentorship are what keep open-knowledge ecosystems alive.
4. **Consistency Outweighs Big Moments**: Long-term impact is built through continuous small contributions, peer reviews, and helping others onboard.
